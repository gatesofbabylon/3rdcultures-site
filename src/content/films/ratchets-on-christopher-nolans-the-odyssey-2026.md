Didn't expect a swords-and-sandals movie to get my mind racing on game theory, Operation Spiderweb and irreversible impairment of the postwar economic order.

And of course, Nolan had Oppenheimer in mind too: one-way doors. The exploit of a tactical advantage, whether a wooden horse or a bomb, as a ratchet that permanently degrades the equilibrium for everyone.

Spiderweb's drones, launched from trucks inside a high-trust logistics system, may in time come to be seen as the thin end of a wedge toward the end of low-friction cross-border trade. Every unexamined container becomes Schrödinger's cat: simultaneously a drone launcher and a case of made-in-China widgets, until someone pays to look. Until everyone has to pay to look.

The end of Zeus' law.

To misquote Boyle's Shallow Grave: "500 pounds is what you paid for it. We don't know how much it cost us yet."
